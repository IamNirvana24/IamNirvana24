## 🛰️ My Journey

I didn't start out chasing Machine Learning — I started out chasing algorithms. Early on, I was convinced that DSA and competitive programming would be the center of my career, the way it is for most CS students.

Then I went deeper into the field, and something shifted. I realized the part that actually pulled me in wasn't just *solving* problems with code — it was understanding how machines could learn patterns from data and make decisions on their own. That distinction changed the direction of everything I did afterward.

DSA hasn't left my life — I still practice it, because it sharpens problem-solving and it's non-negotiable for technical interviews. But it's no longer the center. My real curiosity lives in Machine Learning and AI, and specifically in *how* models work under the hood, not just in importing a library and calling `.fit()`.

Most of what I know didn't come from a classroom — it came from building things, breaking them, reading documentation at 1 AM, and trying again. I like the unglamorous parts of ML just as much as the exciting ones: exploring a messy dataset, cleaning it, engineering features that actually matter, training a model, evaluating it honestly, and then figuring out how to ship it somewhere real.

One of my first real ML experiments captures that curiosity well: a Logistic Regression model that predicted whether a Bollywood movie featured Shah Rukh Khan, built entirely on handcrafted movie features. It wasn't a serious production system — but I integrated `pyttsx3` so the model could *speak* its prediction out loud, and that small detail is what hooked me. Watching a model I built produce an answer, and then hearing that answer spoken back, was the moment I understood how satisfying it is to combine ML with real software.

That small project is the reason I'm now deep into Data Science, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and AI — and still climbing.
